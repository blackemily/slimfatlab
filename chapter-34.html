Alcohol, Sugary Drinks, and Hidden Calories

Drinks can contribute energy without creating the same sense of fullness as many solid foods. Sweetened soda, sweet tea, specialty coffee drinks, juice beverages, energy drinks, and alcoholic drinks can add meaningful calories over the course of a day or week.

Alcohol provides energy and can affect judgment, sleep, appetite, and food choices. Some people find that drinking makes it harder to follow their usual eating intentions. Alcohol also carries health risks that are separate from weight management; less alcohol generally means lower alcohol-related risk, and some people should avoid it entirely.

Read beverage labels and pay attention to serving sizes. A large café drink may contain multiple pumps of syrup, sweetened milk, or toppings. A bottle that appears to be one serving may contain more than one. These details are easy to overlook when drinks are consumed quickly.

Water is a useful everyday choice, but it does not need to be the only drink. Unsweetened tea, sparkling water, and other low- or no-sugar options may help reduce added sugar intake. If you enjoy sweetened beverages, consider smaller sizes or less frequent use rather than assuming an immediate all-or-nothing change is necessary.

People who drink heavily or regularly should speak with a healthcare professional before abruptly stopping, because withdrawal can be dangerous. The safest approach depends on the person's health and drinking pattern.

Key takeaway

Review beverages as part of your overall eating pattern. Small, consistent changes to drinks can reduce added sugar and energy intake.
