Fiber and Its Role in Weight Management

Fiber is a type of carbohydrate found naturally in plant foods. Unlike many digestible carbohydrates, it is not fully broken down in the small intestine. Different fibers have different effects, including supporting bowel regularity, helping manage blood cholesterol, and slowing the absorption of some nutrients.

Fiber-rich foods can support weight management because they often add volume and texture to meals and may help people feel satisfied. Beans, lentils, vegetables, fruit, oats, barley, nuts, seeds, and whole grains are useful sources. Whole foods also provide vitamins, minerals, and other beneficial compounds.

Increasing fiber gradually is usually more comfortable than making a sudden dramatic change. Add one serving at a time and drink adequate fluids. A rapid increase can cause gas, bloating, or changes in bowel habits, especially for people who are not accustomed to higher-fiber meals.

Fiber supplements are available, but they are not identical to eating a varied range of plant foods. Supplements may interact with some medications or affect how they are absorbed, so follow product directions and ask a pharmacist or clinician if you take regular medication.

People with certain digestive conditions may need to adjust the type or amount of fiber they eat. The goal is not to force a universal target but to find a level and mix of foods that support comfort and health.

Key takeaway

Build fiber intake through a variety of plant foods, increase it gradually, and pay attention to digestive comfort.
