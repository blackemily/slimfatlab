Cinnamon and Ginger

Cinnamon and ginger are familiar culinary ingredients with long histories in food preparation. Both contain plant compounds that have been studied for a range of biological effects. Their presence in a supplement may sound reassuring because they are familiar foods, but concentrated extracts are not identical to normal culinary use.

Cinnamon products vary. Cassia cinnamon can contain coumarin, a compound that may be a concern for the liver when consumed in high amounts over time. The type of cinnamon and the amount included are therefore relevant. Ginger can affect digestion for some people and may interact with certain medicines, including medicines that affect blood clotting.

Neither spice should be described as a stand-alone belly-fat solution. In a product evaluation, look for the exact form and quantity, consider personal medication and health circumstances, and avoid taking additional concentrated products simply because the same ingredients appear in the formula.
